The Hemoglobin A1C test (often just called A1C, HbA1c, or the glycohemoglobin test) is a simple blood test that serves as the gold standard for diagnosing and managing prediabetes and diabetes. Unlike a standard finger-prick fasting blood glucose test that only shows your blood sugar at that exact second, the A1C gives you the big picture. It acts like a movie of your blood sugar history rather than a quick snapshot. --- ## How It Works Inside your body, red blood cells carry a protein called **hemoglobin**. When glucose (sugar) enters your bloodstream, it naturally hitches a ride on this protein, a process called glycation. The more sugar in your blood, the more your hemoglobin gets coated with it. Because red blood cells have an average lifespan of about 120 days, measuring this sugar coating provides a remarkably accurate running average of your blood sugar over the last few months. --- ## Understanding Your Score An A1C result is delivered as a percentage. The higher the percentage, the higher your average blood sugar levels have been. Here is how the medical community breaks down the results: * **Normal:** Below 5.7% * **Prediabetes:** 5.7% to 6.4% (Signaling an increased risk of developing full type 2 diabetes) * **Diabetes:** 6.5% or higher on two separate tests > **Note:** For individuals already managing diabetes, a common target is to keep levels **below 7.0%**, though personal goals are often customized by a physician based on age, health history, and individual circumstances. --- ## Why the A1C Test Matters * **No Fasting Required:** Unlike other glucose tests, you can eat, drink, and take your usual medications right up until your blood draw. * **Catches Prediabetes Early:** Prediabetes rarely shows physical symptoms. The A1C test can flag rising blood sugar years before type 2 diabetes develops, providing a crucial window to make lifestyle changes. * **Tracks Treatment Success:** For those managing diabetes, it proves whether a current care plan—including diet, exercise, and medication—is effectively keeping blood sugar stable over time.
